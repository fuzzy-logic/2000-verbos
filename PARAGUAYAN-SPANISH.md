# Learning Paraguayan Spanish

Notes for anyone learning Spanish for Paraguay specifically. Most courses teach a dialect
you won't hear there; this is what's different and what to do about it.

## The dialect

Most courses will teach you Mexican or Peninsular Spanish. Paraguay is neither.

**1. Voseo — learn it instead of `tú`, not after it.**
Paraguay uses `vos`, not `tú`. This is normal educated speech, not slang.

| Standard | Paraguay |
|---|---|
| tú eres | **vos sos** |
| tú tienes | **vos tenés** |
| tú puedes | **vos podés** |
| tú quieres | **vos querés** |
| ven / mira / toma | **vení / mirá / tomá / andá** |

The pattern: take the infinitive, drop the `-r`, stress the last syllable (`hablar → hablás`).
It's easier than `tú` — fewer stem changes. When you pick an online tutor, **filter for
Paraguayan, Argentine or Uruguayan** so you're not drilling a form nobody around you uses.

**2. Guaraní is everywhere.** ~90% of Paraguayans speak Spanish and Guaraní side by side —
Paraguay is the only country where an indigenous language is spoken by the non-indigenous
majority. Everyday speech is *jopara*, Spanish with Guaraní words and particles mixed in.

You are not learning Guaraní to fluency. But **20–30 jopara words buys you more social warmth
than 500 Spanish words will.** Learn `na` (softening/please), `mitã` (kid), `luego` and `ya` in
their local senses, the greetings, the food words. Using them correctly marks you as someone
who's actually here, not passing through.

**3. No yeísmo.** `ll` and `y` are pronounced as *different* sounds here, unlike most of Latin
America. Minor, but it affects your listening — don't be thrown.

## A method that works for this

The short version, for someone starting from zero:

**Language Transfer, *Complete Spanish*** — free, 90 audio lessons, about 14 hours.
It teaches how Spanish *thinks* rather than drilling vocabulary, so you can build sentences
you've never heard. Pause and answer out loud before the recording does; the method falls
apart if you listen passively.

**Comprehensible input** — graded video (Dreaming Spanish and similar) is the volume engine.
Rough milestones by cumulative input hours: 50 for common words to stand out, 150 to follow
learner-adapted content, 300 to follow a patient native speaker, 600 for normal native
speech, 1,500 for practical use.

**Speak earlier than the input-only orthodoxy says.** The "wait 600 hours before speaking"
advice is optimised for someone *not* living in the country. If you're in Asunción, the
social cost of staying silent for a year is far higher than any accent damage from starting
rough. Speak in month one.

**This repo's trainer** fills the vocabulary-and-sentences gap, in the right dialect.

## If you're moving there

The thing that decides your outcome is not method, it's whether Spanish gets into your
actual day. People spend two years in Asunción and leave at A2, not because they chose the
wrong app but because every real interaction happened in English.

- Every service interaction in Spanish from day one, badly. When they switch to English,
  smile and keep going. That's 20+ free reps a week.
- **One recurring hobby in Spanish only** — ideally something physical where low language
  is survivable at first: football, gym, dance, climbing, cooking. Same people, repeatedly,
  with no English escape hatch. This is the single highest-leverage decision.
- A weekly human anchor: one person who speaks only Spanish with you, same time each week,
  who notices if you don't show up.
- Watch who your first five friends are. If they're all anglophones, the next two years are
  largely decided.
- Say it out loud: *"Estoy aprendiendo, hablame en español."* People switch to English to be
  helpful — remove the reason.
